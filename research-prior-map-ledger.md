# Decision ledger from maps #364 and #476 — keep, kill, re-open

Research resolution for [#505](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/505), on map [#502](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/502).

Verdict key — **SURVIVES**: stands as an input to the new journey. **DEAD**: superseded, reverted, or wrong. **RE-OPEN**: must be re-decided on this map.

Two facts about `main` shape most verdicts and are established here once:

- **Phased delivery was removed from the product on 2026-08-14** by [PR #464](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/pull/464) (`refactor!: remove PRD phasing — stories are claimed in design.json`), whose own words are *"The phased-delivery concept ([#370](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/370)) made the spec experience worse… pure boilerplate."* Every phase-bearing decision on [#364](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/364) is therefore already dead in code. Map #364's body still lists phased delivery as a live decision.
- **The only orientation widget either map produced was reverted on 2026-08-05** — commit `0fb284cb` *"revert(console): drop the chat-top interview and design step panels… The panels did not render correctly in use… This reverses the flow-progress part of [#372](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/372)"* — two days after #364 declared MAP COMPLETE (2026-08-03). Nothing replaced it; the map was never re-opened.

## 2. Post-mortem — why the journey did not survive slicing

### The one-sentence cause

**Neither map ever produced a statement of the journey, so "done" meant "no decision is missing" rather than "a person can walk it."** A set of individually correct, individually defensible decisions was declared complete, sliced on mechanism boundaries, and handed to implementation — where every subsequent local correction was also individually correct and the aggregate silently stopped being walkable. There was no artifact any commit, PR, or revert could be checked against, so nothing ever said no.

The proof is a single commit. `0fb284cb` removed the chat-top interview and design step panels — the only "where am I" surface in the product — with an honest, careful message: *"The panels did not render correctly in use. Removing rather than repairing them for now… This reverses the flow-progress part of [#372](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/372). The rest of that resolution stands."* Every word is true and the decision is locally reasonable. It was also the moment the flow was lost, and nothing in the repo, the map, or the issue tracker registered it. Map #364 still lists the stepper decision as live.

### (a) Mechanism-first, not journey-first

The ordering of map #364 is the diagnosis. [#372](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/372) — the only ticket about what the user sees — was **blocked by** [#368](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/368) and [#371](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/371), and was the last substantive ticket resolved. By the time the experience was designed, the artifacts, the write order, the gate semantics and the stage names were frozen. UX was scoped to *render an already-decided mechanism*.

That inversion shows in the artefact itself: [#372](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/372) item 2 defines the user's progress model as *"the design steps checklist (the enforced emission order: cell → scaffold (platform) → enrich → design.md/security.md → openapi/wireframes → criteria)"*. The user's sense of progress was defined as the agent's file-write order. That is not a journey; it is a build log with icons — and it is precisely why removing the widget cost nothing that anyone could name.

Two more:

- [#370](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/370) invented phases to solve a **task-planner scoping** problem ("today the build plans every design component into one milestone"). That mechanism was then pushed into the PRD template, the cell grammar, `design.json`, the build gate, the console spec card, and a user-facing "Start Phase N" CTA. [PR #464](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/pull/464) tore it all out with the verdict *"the phased-delivery concept made the spec experience worse… pure boilerplate"*. A planner's problem became the user's vocabulary and cost the user.
- [#479](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/479) is entirely a build-order decision — three waves, sequenced by which mechanism must exist first (*"per-section grill rides the code-lens infrastructure… building it separately would duplicate the mechanism"*). The one affordance that answers "am I done?" was deliberately scheduled last, because *"it points at doors, so the doors exist first"*. Mechanism dependency ordering beat user-need ordering explicitly, on the record.

### (b) Decisions that assumed behavior no other decision guaranteed

This is the sharpest pattern in the set, and it has a single largest instance.

**Narration.** [#476](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/476)'s charter promised *"live turn narration from the moment the view opens"*; [#483](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/483) and [#485](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/485) specify the actual words — *"Reading your idea… ✓ Consulted org defaults… Planning the interview"* — and build the entire first-run experience on them. Nothing anywhere makes the agent say that. Worse, the *only* decision that ever touched narration went the opposite way: [#373](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/373)'s layer charter replaced the absolute rule with a permission — *"terse by default; a LOADED skill may define the narration for its own flow"* (`services/agents/src/agents/main/prompt.ts:72`). Today exactly one skill exercises that permission, `skills/architecture` (`SKILL.md:322`, dependency narration during the design turn). No skill defines narration for `/start`. So the feature whose entire purpose is to kill 25 seconds of dead air during the interview was specified on top of a system prompt that defaults to one short sentence, and the two maps never noticed because the narration decision lived in the instruction-architecture ticket and the narration *dependency* lived in a console prototype.

Others of the same shape:

- **Timing as a fact.** [#480](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/480) preserves *"design's ~2-minute streak"*; [#485](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/485) is built around *"~25 s until questions appear"*. Both are load-bearing UX premises. Neither map makes latency a contract, and neither specifies what the console shows when it is three times that — which is the state a new developer is most likely to hit.
- **Failure addressing.** [#372](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/372) item 8 justifies its failure cards with *"the enforced order gives failures an address"*. But [#371](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/371) item 6 enforces order at the **`v<N>` tag gate**, not per turn. Nothing guarantees a mid-turn failure carries a step identity for the card to render.
- **A contract named but not decided.** [#477](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/477) says the lossy answer serialization *"must grow a decision-to-question link"* so `*assumed*` tags and the summary card can be derived — a need [#482](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/482) gap 10 had already flagged. The map closed without deciding it. It was then invented during the build (`buildFinishInstruction` in PR #492) and discarded when the PR was closed.
- **Building on a behavior with zero evidence.** [#482](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/482) gap 4 states plainly that `*assumed*` *"has never been exercised: nothing under `evals/spec-agents/` contains it"*. Three features ([#487](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/487) chips, [#490](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/490) counts, [#486](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/486)'s "N asked · M assumed" summary) were then specified on top of skills reliably emitting it. The research ticket found the hole and the resolutions built over it anyway.

### (c) Value gated on other, unbuilt decisions

[#484](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/484) states its own dependency graph: *"1 and 2 are independent and parallelizable; 3, 4, and 5 need 2 (they open sessions); 6 needs 3 (it counts chips)."* Read as a delivery plan, that means five of six issues produce nothing a user can perceive until the whole set lands. There is no intermediate coherence anywhere in the plan and no issue is individually walkable. When 1 and 2 *were* built — PR #493 (6985 additions) and PR #492 (1315 additions) — and both were closed unmerged on 2026-08-17, the set's value went to zero and the tree kept nothing.

The same gating explains why `main` looks the way it does. Everything from map #364 that survives today is exactly the set of decisions that were **self-contained**: prompt strings, steering deletion, the PRD contract, the coverage gate, the scaffold engine, the skill bodies. All of them are invisible to the user. Every user-visible decision sat at the *end* of a dependency chain and has since been reverted (`0fb284cb`), parked ([#377](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/377)'s "follow-up rump" — spec-card CTA and in-document pills, both explicitly deferred), or discarded (#492/#493). The plan's structure guaranteed that when effort ran short, the part that answers "where am I" is the part that gets dropped.

The traceability strand is the cleanest example of the pattern. [#369](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/369) item 6 defers the user-facing lineage view to [#372](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/372); [#372](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/372) item 6 then decides *"no standing coverage panel"*. The platform now computes, on every build, the answer to "did my idea survive?" — and shows it to no one except as a refusal at the gate.

### (d) No journey was stated anywhere, so no issue could violate one

Look at what the two maps declared as their destinations. #364: *"every decision needed to begin implementation is resolved on this map"* — an inventory. #476: *"expressed as console-flow feature issues (ADR-0001) ready to hand to the build flow"* — a queue. Neither destination is an experience, so "complete" was reachable while the flow was undefined, and both maps reached it honestly.

The consequences compound:

1. **The nearest things to journey statements are two claude.ai artifacts** ([#372](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/372), [#483](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/483)) — outside the repo, unversioned, undiffable, unreadable by any agent or reviewer working in the tree. #372's encodes a phase bar and "Start Phase 2" (deleted by [PR #464](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/pull/464)) and a stage rail (reverted by `0fb284cb`). The "locked" reference design describes a product that no longer exists, and nobody noticed because nobody can open it during a code review.
2. **Issues were bound upward, never sideways.** Each of #485–#490 ends with a "Decisions (wayfinder map #476)" block citing *the resolutions that decided it*. None cites a journey step, an entry state, or an exit state. [#484](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/484) then closes the loop the wrong way: *"grilling rounds on those features should sharpen details, not relitigate the map."* The build was instructed to refine mechanisms and forbidden from questioning the shape — which is exactly the check that would have caught the missing flow.
3. **Merged reality never flowed back into the maps.** [PR #464](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/pull/464) deleted phasing from `main` on 2026-08-14. Map #476 was charted on 2026-08-15 and delivered #485–#490 on 2026-08-16 without one mention that roughly a third of map #364's decisions had just been removed from the product. Map #364's "Decisions so far" list has been factually wrong since 2026-08-14 and was handed forward as truth. `0fb284cb` did the same thing to #372 nine days earlier.
4. **The repo already contains the answer, one ADR away, and neither map cites it.** `apps/console/design/decisions/ADR-0014-build-session-spine.md` (2026-07-28, *before both maps*) solved exactly this problem for the build half of the flow: *"A run renders as ONE NUMBERED FLOW, and every applicable stage exists from the moment the run does. A stage that has not been reached says what it waits for; a stage that went wrong says so in the platform's own recorded words"* — with a named actor per stage, because *"naming the actor is what stops a platform wait reading as a hung agent"*. That is a complete orientation model, in the repo, versioned, already shipped. The spec half of the journey re-invented a weaker one (a checklist derived from artifact write order), lost it to a rendering bug, and never looked next door. Only the absence of a single journey doc makes that possible: with one, the two halves of the same walk would have had to agree.

### (e) The measurement regime could not see the failure

Every phase of [#377](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/377) was proved with the chain eval: baseline 63/—/40 → A 85% → B 96% → D 83% → E 91%. Those numbers are real and the agent genuinely got better. But not one phase's proof was a person walking the flow. Phase F — the only user-facing phase — shipped with *"Console suite 631 green"*, a unit-test count, and its central widget was reverted two days later because it *"did not render correctly in use"*. Green tests, rising eval scores, and a lost flow are fully compatible states, and the plan had no instrument that could distinguish them. That is why [#504](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/504) — a human walking the flow and quoting themselves at the moment of confusion — is the correct first move of this map, and why [#502](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/502)'s "Measurement" fog item must not be left unresolved again.

### (f) Maintainer overrides were recorded as decisions without a way back

[#477](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/477) contains two explicit overrides of the session's own recommendation: the docked/split panel was rejected in favour of the full-panel takeover (*"the popup rhythm is a **try-and-see** to revisit against real use"*), and the soft round guard was dropped (*"no length guard"*). Both increase the chance a user loses their place; both were written into the ledger as settled; the "try-and-see" was never scheduled into any issue, eval, or feature. A provisional decision recorded in the same voice as a settled one becomes settled by default — and these two land on the surface where a newcomer is most disoriented.

### What this implies for the new map

1. **Write the journey before anything else, in the repo, versioned.** [#502](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/502) already commits to this; the ledger's evidence is that the *location* matters as much as the content — the two prior "locked" designs failed partly because reviewers could not open them.
2. **Adopt ADR-0014's orientation model across the whole walk**, not a new one: every stage exists from the start, an unreached stage says what it waits for, each stage names its actor.
3. **Make narration and disclosure contracts, not permissions** — [#480](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/480)'s mandatory closing disclosure is the template; row 46 is the hole to close.
4. **Require intermediate coherence per issue** ([#508](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/508)): if an issue can ship without leaving the flow walkable, it is mis-cut. [#484](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/issues/484)'s "3, 4 and 5 need 2" is the anti-pattern to forbid by rule.
5. **Give the journey doc a write-back rule** so a merged revert or a removed concept updates it. Two documented cases (`0fb284cb`, [PR #464](https://github.com/wso2/labs-agentic-engineer/pull/464)) show that without one, the plan is wrong within days and nobody finds out until a walkthrough.
